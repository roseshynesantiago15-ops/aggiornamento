THE FILM

Aggiornamento

In 1962, Pope John XXIII opens the Second Vatican Council as the Catholic Church faces a question that still matters today: How can it stay close to people in a world that keeps changing? Pope Paul VI carries the council forward, and its call for the Church to engage with the modern world reaches far beyond Rome.

Decades later, that question takes on a familiar form. Parishioners share prayers online, watch livestreams when they can’t attend Mass, and use social media to share the Gospel. Moving from the council halls of the 1960s to the phone screens of today, Aggiornamento explores how each generation finds new ways to live and share the faith it has received.

HISTORICAL BACKGROUND

Vatican II & the Modern Church

1963

Inter mirifica

The Council recognized that media such as film, radio, and television could help share the Gospel when used responsibly.

1963

Sacrosanctum concilium

The Council called for the full, conscious, and active participation of the faithful in the liturgy.

1965

Gaudium et spes

The Council asked the Church to engage with contemporary society and interpret the changes of its time in the light of the Gospel.

Reviews

Film critique

By Ramos

Rating ★★★★☆ 4.0/5

What I liked most about Aggiornamento was how it handled tradition. The move from Vatican II to the digital age gave me a clearer picture of why the Church needed to consider new ways of reaching people. I would’ve liked to see more of what that change looked like in an ordinary parish, but the film’s main idea stayed with me. The traditions people grew up with can take on fresh meaning when they’re shared in ways a new generation understands.

By Rivera

Rating ★★★★★ 5.0/5

I’d heard of Vatican II before, but I never really understood why it mattered so much. The film made it easier to see why the Church wanted to listen more closely to people and understand what they were going through. That was the part I connected with most. When people feel heard and welcomed, it’s easier for them to feel like they belong in the Church. I didn’t expect a film about a council from the 1960s to make me think about that.

By Dela Pena

Rating ★★★★★ 5.0/5

The strongest part of Aggiornamento is the way it makes Vatican II feel like the beginning of a new chapter. Moving from the 1960s to the present gives the film room to show just how much daily life has changed since the council. The shift also gives the tagline more weight. By the end, “unchanging faith in an ever-changing world” feels like a question for every generation of Catholics to answer in its own way.

By Jongco

Rating ★★★★★ 5.0/5

The best way I can describe this movie is that the Church has the same heart, and you get to hear its heartbeat in a different rhythm. That’s what I thought about when the story moved into the age of phones and social media. The ways people talk and connect look so different now. It made sense to me that the Church would want to meet people there, too, especially those who might feel far from their parish.

By Cabrera

Rating ★★★★☆ 4.0/5

Aggiornamento got me thinking about the courage it takes to rethink familiar ways of doing things. I would’ve liked to spend a little more time in the Vatican II era because that’s where the questions about change felt most difficult. Still, connecting that part of history to the present worked for me. It made me appreciate how the willingness of people in the past to face hard questions can give people today the courage to do the same.

By Acebuche

Rating ★★★★★ 5.0/5

My favorite detail was how the film’s poster put someone praying right beside someone looking at her phone. It made me think of how normal it feels now to send a livestream link to a relative who can’t make it to Mass or share a prayer in the family group chat. Those are small things, but they can help people feel connected to their parish. That’s what made the jump from Vatican II to our own time feel believable to me.
